Health monitoring for crew in orbit

Team name14–15 November 2026
01

The challenge

Build software that helps astronauts monitor their own health during a space mission.

Microgravity changes bone, muscle, fluid balance and sleep.
Radiation exposure accumulates quietly over a career.
The flight surgeon is on the ground, and the link drops every orbit.
Most health dashboards are designed for mission control, not for the crewmember.

03

Design principles

Your own baseline

Readings are compared with the crewmember’s usual range, not a population norm.

A blank stays a blank

Missing data is shown as “no data”, never as “fine”.

Works without the ground

Everything, including the assistant, runs on board; reports queue for the next signal.

The crewmember owns the record

They choose what the crew sees and what is sent to Earth.

Functions

ExerciseIs my loading enough to hold bone and muscle?
Check-inWhat do I feel that sensors miss?
NoticesWhat needs attention, and what should I do?
RadiationHow much dose today, this mission, this career?
CrewHow is everyone else, at the level they chose to share?
SleepIs my body clock drifting from the schedule?
ReportWhat will my flight surgeon read today?
Ask VigilAn onboard assistant that answers from the crewmember’s own record, with no ground link.
07

Three design directions

Sidebar

Navigation in a quiet left column. Calm and quick to scan.

Top bar

Navigation across the top and the full width for content.

Dark

The same layout on a dark ground, for the pre-sleep period and dim modules.
